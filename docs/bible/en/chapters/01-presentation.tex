\chapter{Introduction}

\section{What \soft is for}
The music of 1980s and 1990s computers and consoles is not recorded. A Commodore~64 music file holds the program the
game ran to play its music; a ZX Spectrum module holds a score and instruments described as register values. To
hear this music as it sounded, you have to run that program on a software copy of the original sound chip. That is
what \soft does, for more than two hundred formats and some fifteen chip families.

We added a second goal: to show you what the chip is doing while you listen. Each voice has its oscilloscope, and
each chip family has a dashboard that shows, frame by frame, its registers, envelopes and waveforms. When a chiptune
puzzles you, you can see how it is built.

\section{Where \soft comes from}
\soft builds on ZXTune, the free player Vitamin created for the ZX Spectrum scene. On that machine every tracker
(Sound Tracker, Pro Tracker, ASC Sound Master, SQ-Tracker\dots) had its own format, and every player read only one.
ZXTune reads them all, finds them inside disk images, archives and packed programs, and plays them back faithfully.
Over the years it took in the best free libraries of other platforms: libsidplayfp for the Commodore~64, Game Music
Emu for consoles, libopenmpt and libxmp for tracker modules, vgmstream for the audio of later games, libvgm for VGM
recordings.

We started from the ZXTune Android application. We kept its playback core, which is solid, and worked on three
things: the fidelity of the emulation engines, visualisation, and ergonomics.

\section{Our choices}
For each chip we use the most accurate engine we have by default, and leave a lighter one to choose for modest
devices. For the Super Nintendo it is the ares engine, which reproduces the sound processor cycle by cycle. For the
PC Engine it is the Ootake emulator's. For Yamaha FM chips it is mostly the Nuked cores, written from a study of
the silicon.

Everything \soft shows is the actual state of the emulated chip at the moment you see it. We do not smooth values
and we do not average them: an average is a value the chip never had. When a graph is enough, such as the SID
filter curve, we add no text to it; when a precise value matters, we write it next to what it describes.

\section{What you need}
\soft runs from Android~4.1. The oscilloscope uses OpenGL ES, or Vulkan if your phone supports it, which makes it
smoother. The application takes about 16~MB. Online catalogues need a connection, but tracks you have already played
stay in the cache.
