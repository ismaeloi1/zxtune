\chapter{Chip dashboards}\label{chap:dashboards}

Below the oscilloscope, the dashboard details the chip state. Each chip has its own view, built around what is
specific to its synthesis. State snapshots are taken about every millisecond of emulated sound; each frame shows the
latest one, without smoothing.

\section{SID (Commodore 64)}
\capture[0.8]{sidcells-now-playing}{SID dashboard}
For each voice: waveform and duty cycle, envelope (level, phase, ADSR in milliseconds), frequency (note and hertz).
The \textbf{Mod} column shows sync and ring modulation with the source voice: ``Sync V3'', ``Ring V2'', and
``Sync+Ring V1'' when both are on. On the SID a voice is always modulated by the previous one (V1 from V3, V2 from V1,
V3 from V2). The last row shows volume, resonance and filter.
\capturedouble{sidcells-dual}{sidcells-fullscreen}{Two SID chips}{fullscreen}

\section{Super Nintendo (S-DSP)}
\capturedouble{snes}{snes-inspector}{View of the 8 voices}{voice inspector}
For each voice: sample, frequency, drawn ADSR or GAIN envelope, left and right volumes, echo, noise, pitch
modulation. At the bottom: master volumes, echo and its FIR filter. Tapping a voice opens its inspector.

\section{PC Engine (HuC6280)}
\capture[0.8]{pce-dashboard}{PC Engine view}
For each of the 6 voices: the 32-step wavetable in use, a level bar and two panning bars, then note, frequency and
level. The waveform shape is named (sine, triangle, rising saw, falling saw, square, pulse with its duty cycle) and
each distinct table gets a number, which changes when the instrument changes. The bottom row gives the registers as
written (\$802 to \$807); the global row, \$801, \$808 and \$809. The HuC6280 has no reverse playback: a falling saw is
a table written backwards by the driver, hence ``Saw $\downarrow$ (reversed)''.

\section{FM chips}
\capturedouble{fm-megadrive-cards}{fm-megadrive-inspector}{Mega Drive: voice cards}{voice inspector}
\capturedouble{fmview-algorithms}{fm-adlib-cards}{FM algorithms}{AdLib}
Each voice shows its algorithm (carriers and modulators), levels and envelopes of its operators. Tapping a voice opens
the inspector: drawn envelopes, multipliers, detune, LFO.
