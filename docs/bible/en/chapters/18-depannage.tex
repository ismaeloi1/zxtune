\chapter{Troubleshooting}

Most problems we, or the first users, ran into are solved in a few seconds. Here are the most common, with what we
check first.

\section{The sound crackles or drops out}
Look at the ``render'' line of the statistics. Below 100~\%, your phone is not computing the music fast enough.
Choose a lighter engine (chapter~\ref{chap:emu-settings}): Genesis Plus GX instead of Nuked for the Mega Drive,
snes\_spc instead of ares for the Super Nintendo. If the line is well above 100~\%, the problem is more likely the
drawing: lower the oscilloscope frame rate or switch to Vulkan rendering.

Also check that no battery saver is throttling the application. Some manufacturers slow down background apps even
while they play sound.

\section{The oscilloscope stutters}
Turn on Vulkan if it is offered. On tracks with very many lanes (a Sega Model~2 board can show around thirty),
reduce the number of periods shown: triggering gets cheaper.

\section{A file is not recognised}
Look for its extension in the tables of part~\ref{part:compat}. Rips of the xSF family (PSF, SSF, GSF, SNSF,
2SF\dots) are often split into two files: the tiny track itself, and a \texttt{.\_lib} library holding the code and
sounds. Both must be in the same folder.

\section{The track is too short or too long}
Many formats give no duration, because the music loops forever. Set the default duration or the loop limit. For SID,
the duration comes from the HVSC database; a track missing from it gets the default duration.

\section{Notes are wrong on AY music}
The chip clock does not match the original machine. A ZX Spectrum 128 runs at 1.7734~MHz, an Amstrad CPC at 1~MHz,
an Atari ST at 2~MHz. A wrong clock shifts the whole track by the same interval: the tune stays recognisable, but
everything is too high or too low.

\section{No sound on an MT-32 track}
MT-32 emulation needs the original module's memory images, which we cannot supply. Without them the track stays
silent.

\section{The online catalogue does not respond}
Catalogues are third-party services that can be offline. Tracks you already played stay in the cache and play without
a connection.

\section{My old playlists are gone}
Old XSPF playlists are still in the browser. Open them and add their tracks to a playlist.

\section{Reporting a problem to us}
Give us the application version, your phone model, the file concerned and, if a setting is involved, its internal key
(chapter~\ref{chap:all-settings}). A screenshot of the statistics lines saves us a lot of time.
